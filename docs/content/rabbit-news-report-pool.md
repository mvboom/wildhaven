## Species: European rabbit (*Oryctolagus cuniculus*) — human decision

Not the eastern cottontail, and the choice is load-bearing rather than cosmetic. Cottontails
are solitary and mutually intolerant and shelter in a scrape; European rabbits are social and
live in warrens. **Cottontail would have deleted the safe copy territory** — a solitary animal
leaves little to write about but locomotion and vigilance, and both are predation tropes in
costume. Warren life is home-building and family.

Accepted cost: the US-native wild rabbit is the cottontail, and *O. cuniculus* is introduced
and ecologically problematic across much of its range. US register is preserved in *language*,
not in species nativeness.

## Terminology: "baby rabbits", never "kits" or "kittens"

Both standard terms collide with the roster: **"kits" is already used in the shipped Fox fact
card**, and **"kittens" collides with Cat**. "Bunnies" is acceptable in ambient flavor (it is
register, not a factual claim) but stays out of the fact card.

Generalizable rule this implies: **check new species terminology against the whole roster, not
just against the source.** Add to the register rules in gdd.md alongside US-English.

## Sub-pools

### Discovery / hint — pre-move-in, soft-hints `open_grass`

- "Word has it a group of rabbits is looking for a new home — somewhere with wide open grass to graze…"
- "A rabbit was seen out in the meadow, sizing it up as if measuring it for tunnels."
- "Rumor from the hedgerow: rabbits want a big grassy spot with room for a whole warren…"

### Move-in announcement

- "Rabbits have moved into the meadow! They've started a warren under the grass."
- "Good news from the open grass: a group of rabbits has settled in — and there are more of them than you'd think."

### Ambient / flavor — also usable as welcome-back lines

- "The rabbits came out to graze as the sun went down."
- "Someone counted eight rabbits out on the grass this evening — a whole warren's worth."
- "There's a new tunnel entrance out in the meadow that wasn't there last week."
- "The rabbits have been at the garden greens again."
- "The young rabbits stayed close to the warren all evening."
- "Whoever saw the rabbits today says no two are quite the same shade of brown." — pairs with the `variation_seed` coat tint; reads correctly before *and* after that lands.

**Cut during drafting, recorded so the reasoning survives:** *"A rabbit sat up tall in the
grass with its long ears turned every which way."* The posture is real, but it is the
ears-as-early-warning trope with the threat merely left offscreen, and every source states the
behavior in explicitly predation-framed terms. Cut rather than laundered.

### Avoidance relocation — rabbit side

## Verified sources

- [ADW — *Oryctolagus cuniculus*](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Oryctolagus_cuniculus/) — social groups of ~6–10 adults; warrens; nocturnal, foraging evening until morning; herbivorous
- [ADW — Lagomorpha](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Lagomorpha/) — incisors grow throughout life
- [The Wildlife Trusts — Rabbit](https://www.wildlifetrusts.org/wildlife-explorer/mammals/rabbit) — highly social; grassland/farmland/gardens; greyish-brown coat
