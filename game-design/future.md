# Wildhaven — Future Work

Deferred-but-designed ideas, and everything beyond v1's fifteen Tier-1
requirements ([gdd.md](gdd.md) → Scope). Rule: **nothing gets cut from a
design pass without landing here.** One entry per idea: design intent in a
line or two, plus what it's waiting on.

## Next in line (former Tier 2)

| Idea | Design intent | Waiting on |
|---|---|---|
| **Read-Aloud full coverage** | Extend the OS-level TTS Read-Aloud button beyond v1's thin slice (fact cards and the displacement warning) to News Reports, Field Guide entries, and every future text — zero per-line production cost since it rides the OS's own text-to-speech. Accepted tradeoff: robotic, OS-dependent voice quality, with Linux TTS less consistent than Windows/Mac — acceptable for the zero-maintenance win. | v1 build hours, if ahead of schedule; default on/off state still open |
| **Identity slice** | Naming (any individual, villagers included, no per-species limit), a moved-away registry, and right of first return: a named individual that moved away is remembered in the save and returns as *that individual* — name and bond intact — when its species next finds habitat and capacity admits an arrival. The registry grants identity priority in the arrival queue, never a capacity override — Layla comes back first, not extra. | v1 build hours; the save format's `save_version` field is what lets these two fields (`name`, `bond_level`) and a lookup be added later without breaking old saves |
| **Milestone badges** | Purely celebratory badges marking bond/collection milestones ("Ferra trusts you now!") — nothing depends on them (10 badges is exactly as good as 100, the indicator test). Generic by design: a badge is a (tracked counter, threshold, message) data entry, so new milestones require no code. | v1 build hours, if ahead of schedule |
| **Scouts** | An active Discovery accelerator: once the player has a Villager, a button on a cooldown (never a resource cost, which would read as pay-to-progress) guarantees a News Report biased toward animals relevant to terrain already built. **Hard constraint: Scouts must never be the only realistic way to discover or attract an animal** — passive scouting is always fully sufficient; Scouts only accelerate. | v1 build hours, once News Reports exist; cooldown length and Field Guide pacing still open |
| **Standalone Tutorial screen** | A dedicated onboarding screen beyond v1's first-time nudge, which covers minimum onboarding on its own. | v1 build hours, if ahead of schedule; format open — static images vs. recorded clips |
| **Fences, birdhouses & the Shed** | Small placeables beyond the House: Fence and Birdhouse (leaning toward any land terrain except water — final list still open), and a Shed placeable that retains the House's 1×1 thin-form asset once the House deepens to its full 2×2 footprint, so the thin build's asset is never throwaway work. | v1 build hours, if ahead of schedule; final allowed-terrain list per placeable |
| **Tag-driven ambient audio layers + proximity mix** | v1 ships one ambient bed plus soft confirmation SFX. The designed-but-deferred system layers ambient audio by the same habitat-tag vocabulary that drives terrain and animal needs, crossfading by camera zoom level — the original design, viable again since D-41 restored the zoom continuum (briefly flagged as needing a distance-to-source redesign during D-33's first-person interlude, when zoom did not exist as a camera axis; that flag no longer applies). | v1 build hours, if ahead of schedule |
| **Charm layer** | The ambient-life two-layer system: songbird flocks, butterflies, fish ripples and dragonflies, fireflies — cheap, numerous flock/particle logic with no pathfinding, needs, or persistence, not tappable, spawning purely from local habitat quality (wordless feedback on habitat quality; spawn-rate and culling tuning still open). Alongside it: per-individual variation (a persistent random seed driving coat tint within species-safe palettes, a pattern mask, and ±10% size — stored as `variation_seed`, setting up the naming/bond layer), a shared behavior library (napping in the sun, stretching, a two-animal chase, follow-the-leader, a curious look-at-camera reward), and building variants and other living touches (Townscaper-style building joining is its own entry, below). "Culled at far zoom" and "only triggers at close zoom" are viable again as originally specified — D-41 restored the zoom continuum after D-33's first-person interlude (which had no far-zoom state during normal play) briefly required re-expressing these as proximity-to-player instead. | v1 build hours; ambient life layer proving out cheap enough at the v1 performance ceiling |
| **Welcome-back moments** | Session-start flavor messages drawn from the same News Reports pool, optionally pointing somewhere. **Hard constraint: tone and warmth are identical regardless of how long the player was away** — no guilt, no escalating absence rewards, no gap-length awareness. | v1 build hours, if ahead of schedule |
| **Guaranteed arrival showcase & guaranteed-baseline rule** | Any animal's first move-in is guaranteed visibly active for that session (the fact-card moment happens in the open, regardless of shyness) — the payoff for a hard animal is never anticlimactic. A lived-in stretch always shows at least a small handful of Bold/common animals visibly active near their homes, so a settled area never looks empty by bad luck; Shy/rare sightings layer on top as bonus. | v1 build hours; cheap enough to be an early candidate if time allows |
| **Field Guide polish & per-species detail pages** | v1 ships a flat, resident-state-only species list in the Field Guide. Deferred depth is a richer detail page per animal, beyond the flat list. | v1 build hours, if ahead of schedule |
| **Per-species News Report pools & outward-pointing mist invitations** | v1 ships one small shared News Report pool. Deferred depth splits it into per-species pools (sharper, more specific hints) and adds a few invitations that point outward toward the mist edge — still never metering progress, only inviting. | v1 build hours, if ahead of schedule |

## Later (former Tier 3)

| Idea | Design intent | Waiting on |
|---|---|---|
| **Companionship interaction layer** | Pet/Wave/Feed and bond levels, beyond the identity slice above. A small extensible interaction set (v1-scope target: 2–3, all free), each defined by an `InteractionDefinition` — `id` (Pet, Wave, Feed, …), `animation` (reaction reference), `bond_gain` (bond-level increment), and an optional resource cost field, free in v1 but designed to later carry a cost under the deferred multi-resource economy. **Feeding is purely additive, never required** — an animal never fed is exactly as happy and present as one that is. A bond level is a record of a friendship, not a meter: no maximum, gates nothing — one deeply-bonded fox and twenty casual acquaintances are equally valid. | Identity slice (naming) landing first |
| **Time-of-day activity & finer visibility rules** | Some animals more visible at certain times (nocturnal species at night) — flavor and variety only, layered on the cosmetic day/night cycle; never gates suitability. Refines the always-on Bold-often-open/Shy-mostly-cover visibility split with finer time-based rules. | Day/night cycle landing first; playtesting whether it earns its complexity beyond the guaranteed-baseline rule |
| **Day/night cycle & local climate cosmetics** | A global, cosmetic-only lighting shift — no spawning or danger gating — with the ambient soundscape crossfading to a night layer (crickets replacing birdsong). "Seasons" are static local climate, not a rotating calendar: each area's cosmetic weather (heat shimmer, drifting snow, gentle rain) is driven by its dominant terrain tags, purely cosmetic with zero mechanical impact — a wintery corner beside a desert is fine by design. True rotating seasons are a further-out post-class stretch (see below), not this. | v1 build hours, if ahead of schedule |
| **Local weather particles** | Per-area cosmetic weather effects (heat shimmer, drifting snow, gentle rain) tied to dominant terrain tags. | Day/night cycle & local climate cosmetics landing first |

## Deferred & cut ideas

| Idea | Design intent | Waiting on |
|---|---|---|
| **Townscaper-style building joining** | Adjacent same-type buildings merge and re-style (houses become row-houses) so placement itself is the toy — fence runs and merged farm fields via autotiling. Deferred whole: v1 ships neither fence runs nor autotiled field merging. | Building-variant art and the autotiling system, both post-class |
| **Player decoration layer** | Small free/cheap props (flags, lanterns, flower pots) kids sprinkle to make places theirs. | Interaction-model review — must stay inside "pick a mode, then tap" |
| **Full picture-book re-model pass** | Re-model existing assets to the picture-book guidelines — rounder silhouettes, proportionally larger heads and eyes, a soft two-step toon ramp, a saturated-but-gentle palette, every animal readable as a friendly shape at the default mid-zoom (D-41). v1 applies the guidelines to new assets only; existing assets are not retrofitted. | Post-class art budget |
| **Ambient-life interactions** | Ambient creatures react to residents — birds scatter when a fox trots past. Pure charm, no mechanics. | Ambient life layer (Charm layer, above) shipping first |
| **Hero named characters** | A small cast of bespoke individuals with unique looks and stronger reactions (Animal Crossing-villager energy), beyond the generated per-individual variation. | Naming/bond system landing first |
| **Expansion past the 128×128 cap** | Let thriving worlds keep growing beyond the v1 performance ceiling — needs streaming/culling work the v1 renderer won't have. | Post-v1 performance headroom |
| **Multi-resource economy** (absorbs Stone & the Quarry) | The original economy: forest→Wood, field→Food, rock→Stone, feeding amenities that require different materials, so a mixed landscape is a *material* incentive and not only a habitat one — the full three-resource economy feeding material-differentiated amenities. v1 ships one resource (Wood) as a pacer — a material, not a score — and rests the mixed-landscape incentive on habitat diversity and carrying capacity instead. Pairs with the Species amenities entry below: material-differentiated and species-targeted amenities are the same feature from two sides. | Post-class scope; cost-table rebalance when added |
| **Harmony Mode** (cut toggle) | A mode that skips avoids/compatibility checks for kids who want a big happy mixed group. Cut because the base game is already nearly harmony (avoids never gates move-in; worst case is a gentle announced relocation), and a live toggle has a structural eviction hazard: switching Harmony→Habitat makes previously-legal arrangements into violations and "corrects" a bonded world. **Return condition:** only if kid playtesting (step 5+) shows the avoids puzzle frustrates the young end — and then as a per-world choice fixed at creation, never a live toggle; try generous avoid-distance tuning first. | Kid-playtest evidence of avoids frustration |
| **Mutualism / "Likes" pairing** (considered, not pursued) | A symmetric opposite to Avoids: pairs that cluster deliberately — e.g. a domestic-cat-type species avoiding Fox while several small species avoid the cat in turn — plus a general "Likes" tag for mutualistic pairs (Human + a prospective Cat). **Not pursued for v1:** no cat, or any comparable small-predator species, exists in the roster or cleared pool, so the concrete pairing has no species to attach to yet; a general Likes mechanic is a new system (a clustering bonus, its own tuning, UI surfacing) with no Tier-1 slot and no named forfeit per the Change Control rule (gdd.md → Scope). | A predator-coded species entering the cleared pool, or kid-playtest evidence that clustering incentives are missing |
| **Species amenities** (absorbs the cut Village Happiness + Amenity Buildings) | Amenities return *inside* the species model, for **every** species: a special placeable that delights or attracts a particular species — a Well/School/Market for villagers exactly as a birdhouse is for birds, or something special a kid gives a fox. Driven by the existing tag/delight systems (species-targeted emitted tags, or bond-delight like Feed a Treat). Village Happiness as a meter is dropped for good — it was the one mechanic not derivable from villagers-are-a-species. | Post-class; tag-vocabulary review per new amenity |
| **Cosmetic variation in revealed land** | Give newly revealed mist land purely aesthetic, habitat-**neutral** texture (a rocky outcrop, a sandy patch) instead of plain wild grass, for visual interest. v1 reveals flat wild grass — visually grass-family but tag-inert, so it emits no habitat tags and contributes nothing to qualification or carrying capacity. Any added variation must stay tag-neutral so revealing never hands the player free habitat (the mist no-reward pillar). | Playtest showing plain-grass reveal reads as dull |
| **Per-terrain visual variety at scale** | v1's terrain tiles (forest, rock) each wire a single imported prop into `TerrainDefinition.model_scene`, so every painted tile of a given terrain instances the identical mesh — fine at a glance, but a large stretch of adjacent Forest or Rock tiles risks reading as visibly repetitive (same tree, same boulder, tiled). A second CC0 variant is already imported and sitting unused for both (`CommonTree2.tscn`, and Nature Pack's `Rock_2..7`/`Rock_Moss_*` for rock) — deferred work is wiring in per-tile variant selection (random or position-seeded) rather than sourcing new assets. Noted 2026-08-03 during the grass/forest/rock grey-box-to-real-asset swap; not a v1 blocker, flagged for a future look pass. | Playtest or human look-pass showing tiled repetition actually reads as flat/artificial at typical build density |
| **Accessibility options** | Font/UI text scaling, a reduce-motion toggle (dampens ambient-life and weather particles plus camera-ease animations), a dyslexia-friendly font, a color-independence audit (terrain, habitat tags, and mode states legible by shape/icon/label, not hue alone — ~1-in-12 boys have a color-vision deficiency), and generous touch-friendly hit-target sizing. The audience (kids 6–10, often beside a parent) makes these genuinely valuable and they're cheap in Godot's theme system, but they're outside the Tier-1 core loop. **Read-Aloud narration is *not* here** — it ships as a thin slice in v1 itself (fact cards and the displacement warning), because it is the audience-reach lever, not a generic add-on; only Read-Aloud's *full coverage* is the deferred item, listed separately above. | Post-class, or pulled forward if build velocity allows |
| **Full accessibility (v2)** | Input remapping and menu/UI screen-reader semantics. | Rides the v2 Android/iOS tablet + touch-input pass |

## Post-class direction

Rough priority order for what comes after the class deliverable ships.

| Idea | Design intent | Waiting on |
|---|---|---|
| **Photo Mode** | Photograph animals with an in-game album (reuses the save-thumbnail screenshot code). First thing to consider after the class. | Post-class, first priority |
| **Individual trait variance** | A per-individual shyness stat that shifts with bonding — a well-bonded Shy animal becomes more visible. Top-priority stretch for the "hardest animal to see" anticlimax risk. | Post-class |
| **Population-level growth** | A world/species-level counter ("3 of your 8 cougars are cubs") using scaled models and higher-pitched cues instead of separate baby assets. Per-individual life stages were rejected (doubles asset cost per animal). | Post-class |
| **True rotating seasons** | Visual variety over time, replacing the static local-climate cosmetics with a rotating calendar. | Post-class; local climate cosmetics (above) landing first |
| **Roster growth** | Occasional new animals as a cheap "something new" hook — the long-term replayability answer. Each addition is one Add-an-Animal pipeline run, which keeps it cheap forever. | Post-class |
| **Deeper interactive play** | Fetch/toy minigames — needs real per-animal behavior trees. | Post-class; the behavior library (Charm layer, above) landing first |
