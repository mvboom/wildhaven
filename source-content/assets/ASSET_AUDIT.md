## Decisions locked

- **Base ecosystem: Quaternius, all the way.** CC0, Godot-native (glTF), one creator →
  cohesion for free, and the AI-driven pipeline has the least friction on glTF. This is the
  standard; we don't mix creators for hero content.
- **"Make it my own" = looks unique, not owned.** Delivered by a **look-pass layer** — a
  uniform toon/cel + rim shader, a Wildhaven palette, lighting + post-processing, ambient FX —
  applied over the assets. This is what AI agents *can* do and it's base-agnostic. **Mesh work
  (re-topo, re-proportion, cute-ification) is NOT available** (no in-house art; the human
  mesh contact is a pinch-hitter, not a pipeline), so **out-of-the-box silhouettes ship as-is** —
  pick assets whose forms we're happy to ship.
- **Sanctioned paid fallback: Synty SIMPLE (animals only).** If a gap species has no animated
  Quaternius model, a Synty **SIMPLE** animal is an acceptable buy — it's the closest paid
  line to Quaternius and blends under the shared shader. **Animals only; humans stay
  Quaternius** (character style is where cross-creator mismatch shows). Never Synty POLYGON
  (off-brief detail; and its line has almost no animals anyway).

### What the sweep found instead

Asked why the shipped houses look poor, the audit found the answer inside **already-cleared
packs**, not in any new download:

- **`Ultimate Fantasy RTS` ships 18 house variants; only the 9 FirstAge ones had been
  imported.** The shipped `Houses_FirstAge_1_Level1` is a **404-triangle, 2-material lean-to
  with no modelled door or windows** — that is why it reads poor. The SecondAge tier is the
  same pack, artist and palette at 964–5,948 tris and 5–6 materials, with modelled doors and
  windows, in native glTF under the existing attribution entry. Three of them
  (`SecondAge_1_Level1` 0.890×0.864, `_2_Level1` 0.887×0.811, `_3_Level1` 0.677×0.677) fit a
  1×1 tile **unscaled**. Imported 2026-08-29 → `project/assets/buildings/house_secondage_*`.
  `_2_Level2` and `_2_Level3` are imported but **deliberately not wired**: they are
  multi-building compounds that flatten to 0.53 and 0.43 tile-heights inside a 1×1 footprint,
  and a variant cannot declare its own footprint (human ruling, 2026-08-29).
- **`Animated Men/Women Characters - Feb 2019` were never unusable — they were never looked
  at.** The shipped `human_man/Man.glb` **is** `Male_Casual` from the men's pack, re-downloaded
  from poly.pizza as a standalone `.glb` (verified material-by-material: 318/892/96/28/222
  triangles identical, same `BaseHuman` mesh, same `HumanArmature` rig, same 11 clips). Its 15
  siblings sat unexamined. All carry 11 real clips on the rig that already ships; eye-height
  ratios (0.924–0.932) sit inside the shipped band and are *more* stylized than `Adventurer`
  (0.943), the default villager. 13 imported 2026-08-29 → `project/assets/animals/human_*`;
  `Smooth_` is a subdivision variant (identical silhouette, all-quad, ~4× tris) and **both
  sets are wired deliberately for an in-engine comparison** — the losing set gets pulled.
  **`Male_Suit`/`Smooth_Male_Suit` held out**: `Kd 0.0185` on shirt and pants is effectively
  pure black, excluded by art.md's palette rule.

**The lesson, recorded because it cost twice:** `assessed` in the manifest means *"we know what
is in this pack"*, **not** *"we decided against it"*. `scripts/asset-manifest.py --unused` now
reports each cleared pack's imported/unused split for exactly this reason. As of this sweep:
`Ultimate Fantasy RTS` 11/128, `Ultimate Nature Pack` 17/150, `Stylized Nature MegaKit` 4/68.

### Farm Animals pack — FBX exports are incomplete (2026-08-30)

Investigated because Pig's FBX carries only `Idle` and `Jump` while the pack preview shows a
walking pig. **The clips are not mislabeled** — measured in-engine, `Armature|Jump` has both
hind legs peaking at the same phase (108° at 11/24 — in phase, not antiphase) in a single
anticipate-leap-land burst, which is a genuine hop, not a gait. `Idle` has zero foot rotation
at all.

**The walk exists in the `.blend` and did not survive the FBX export.** `Blends/Pig.blend`
contains `ACIdle`, `ACJump`, `ACRun`, `ACWalk`, `ACWalkSlow`; the FBX has two of the five.
Pack-wide, comparing each FBX's AnimationStacks against its `.blend`:

| Model | FBX clips | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Cow, Horse, Zebra | Idle, Jump, Run, Walk, WalkSlow, Death | complete |
| **Pig, Sheep, Llama, Pug** | **Idle, Jump only** | **truncated — Walk/Run recoverable from the `.blend`** |

All seven `.blend` files carry Walk and Run actions. The OBJ variants carry no animation at
all (format limitation), so **re-exporting from the `.blend` in Blender is the only recovery
path** — Blender is not installed on this machine. `scripts/asset_pipeline.py`'s audit stage
correctly refuses Pig ("missing required animation clip(s) Walk -- a model with no walk cycle
cannot roam"); that gate is doing its job and should not be worked around.

This settles the standing note below — "**Farm Animals** pack is presumed rigged but its title
lacks 'Animated' — confirm animations exist before relying on those five". Confirmed, and more
specific: rigged and animated yes, but four of seven FBX exports are lossy. Note also that
`Ultimate Animated Animals` contains **no Pig** (Alpaca, Bull, Cow, Deer, Donkey, Fox, Horse,
Horse_White, Husky, ShibaInu, Stag, Wolf), so there is no already-cleared substitute on disk.

## Sourcing pipeline notes

- **Formats:** prefer **glTF** (Ultimate Animated Animals, MegaKit, RTS ship it). Character,
  Farm-Animal, Nature, Trees, Crops packs are OBJ/FBX/Blend — import FBX for animated ones.
  poly.pizza serves FBX/GLTF.
- **poly.pizza, filtered to Quaternius,** is the standing catalog to re-check for the missing
  animals as it grows. Verify each candidate's `#Animated` tag *on its model page* — the search
  view under-reports it.
